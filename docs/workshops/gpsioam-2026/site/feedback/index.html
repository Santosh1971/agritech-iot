GPSIOAM · Farm IoT workshop · After the workshop

What the students said after

At the end of the day, each student filled in a short form on their phone: how useful the day was, what they liked, what was hard, whether they would use a college IoT lab, and what they want to build next.

All 11 students responded (3rd year, B.Sc (Hons) Agriculture) · 1 October 2026 · Bars show how many students chose each option (more than one could be chosen). Quotes are anonymised.

The day

Every student rated the day 4 or 5 out of 5

4.7 / 5average usefulness: 8 students gave 5, 3 gave 4, none lower
4.5 / 5interest in starting their own business after the day (6 gave 5, 5 gave 4)
8 of 11would use a college IoT lab regularly; the other 3 for projects
10 of 11would happily teach their juniors

Biggest change: one student who rated business interest 1 out of 5 before the workshop rated it 4 after it.

In their words

What worked, and what was hard

Liked most

  • Making their own phone app and controlling the board from it (4 students)
  • Sensors doing what they command, running their own program (4)
  • Programming with AI and learning the sensors (1)
  • Hands-on building (1)

Found difficult

  • Wiring and new components: remembering sensor parts and connections (4)
  • Confusing at first, easy and fun after a few tries (2)
  • The pace: a lot to take in one day (2)
  • Network problems in the room (1)
“Developing our own app and using it to control the controller.”
“Starting the coding felt a little difficult; after many trials it felt easy.”
“In the beginning it was confusing, later it started turning into fun.”
“Making the LED blink as per our command.”

What this means: the moment a device obeys a student’s own command, or appears as their own app, is what won them over. Wiring is the weak spot. More practice time with the hardware, ideally weekly, is the fix the students themselves asked for.

What they asked of us

  • A longer training programme: “in one day we can’t learn everything”
  • Internships after the degree (2)
  • Low-cost technology that small farmers can use (2)
  • Building new products together with the facilitators
  • Their own ideas represented and implemented

What this means: the two main blockers, “no time” and “don’t know where to start”, are exactly what a lab with fixed weekly hours and ready project briefs removes. Ten of eleven are willing to teach juniors, which is the seniors-teach-juniors model of the lab proposal.

Next steps

Ten new projects from their ideas, on the same kit

We turned the students’ ideas into ten ready projects. Each uses only the kit they already have (ESP32, temperature–humidity sensor, water-flow sensor, pump relay and clock). They are now on the workshop flasher page with the student’s name on each, so students can continue from home or in the lab with Claude.
