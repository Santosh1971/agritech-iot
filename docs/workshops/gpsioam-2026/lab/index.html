Agri IoT Innovation Lab

Imagine a lab on campus where you go from your first sensor reading in Year 1 to running your own agri-tech venture by Year 4. You build farm devices with the help of AI, test them on the college farm and with real farmers, and learn everything that turns a working prototype into a product that sells: design, manufacturing, pricing, marketing and funding. This is an idea we are exploring with your college, and we want to know what you would expect from it.

Why a lab

Agriculture needs graduates who can build, not only use, farm technology

Theory without practice

Students study wireless sensors, monitoring systems, IoT devices and drones, but rarely build or program one themselves.

AI lowers the barrier

With AI assistants, an agriculture student can program a device and build a phone app by describing what the farm needs. Crop knowledge becomes the advantage.

Industry is hiring

Agri-tech companies (sensor networks, advisories, precision irrigation, drones) need people who understand both the crop and the device.

Founders, not only employees

A student who has piloted a device with real farmers has what investors and incubators ask for: a validated problem and a working product.

Four years, four steps

From first exposure to entrepreneur

Designed for the four-year B.Sc (Hons) Agricultural Skill and Entrepreneurship programme, whose focus on entrepreneurship matches the lab’s goal. Each year builds on the one before. Activities run as lab sessions, a student club and project work alongside the regular courses, and connect to the ICAR programmes students already take. The college’s diploma and PG programmes can join later as shorter modules.

Year 1

Discover

See what farm technology can do, and make a device work for the first time.

Students do
Read a temperature-humidity sensor in a free simulator, then on a real board; see readings on a phone; field visits to our deployed devices.
Output
A working sensor reading and a first phone screen.
Time
About 6 sessions a year, plus the club.
Year 2

Build

Build complete monitoring systems and use the data for agronomic decisions.

Students do
Weather, soil moisture, water flow and storage monitors; cloud dashboards; phone apps built with AI; link readings to what they learn in agronomy, plant pathology and entomology (disease risk, degree-days, irrigation need).
Output
A prototype running on the college farm, with a month of data.
Time
A semester project plus workshops.
Year 3

Solve

Find a real farmer problem and take a device from prototype toward product.

Students do
Interview farmers and dealers; pilot devices on partner farms; design for the field (solar power, enclosures, surge protection); a first circuit board; costing; the AGR 322 workshop and an annual Agri-IoT hackathon.
Output
A field-tested device with farmer feedback and a cost sheet.
Time
Course work, hackathon and a field pilot.
Year 4

Launch

Turn the product into a venture during the final-year experiential programmes.

Students do
Use RAWE and the Experiential Learning Programme (ELP) to run a small production batch, sell to farmers or FPOs, set pricing and service, and pitch to incubators; internships with agri-tech companies, including ours.
Output
A pilot batch in farmers’ hands, a business plan and an incubation application.
Time
ELP / RAWE semester.
A culture that lasts

Seniors teach juniors

The lab should keep running long after we step back. The way to get there is for every batch to teach the batch below it. Teaching also deepens the seniors’ own skills, and it is the best training for the founders and team leaders they want to become.

Year 4 mentors Year 3Product and venture

Share pilots, costing, dealer and farmer lessons; review Year 3 field designs.

Year 3 mentors Year 2Field and design

Take Year 2 teams on farm pilots; teach enclosures, power and board design.

Year 2 teaches Year 1Build sessions

Run the Year 1 Discover sessions as teaching assistants, with our support.

Year 1 learnsFirst devices

Joins the club, helps maintain the living lab, and gets ready to teach next year.

Activities

  • Buddy teams: every junior team has a senior buddy for its project
  • Student-run sessions: seniors teach the Year 1 and Year 2 labs; faculty and we observe and coach
  • Open lab hours: two evenings a week, staffed by seniors on a rota
  • Apprenticeships: juniors join seniors’ farmer pilots and hackathon teams
  • Monthly show-and-tell: mixed-year teams present what they built and what failed
  • Hand-over day: at year end, each batch hands its living-lab devices, kits and open projects to the next

Student lab committee

  • Lab lead: runs the lab calendar and the committee
  • Kit keeper: inventory, issue and return of kits, repairs
  • Field lead: keeps the living-lab devices on the farm running
  • Knowledge lead: keeps the lab handbook and project library up to date
  • Outreach lead: farmer demos, hackathon and industry links
  • Chosen each year; each role hands over to a junior deputy

Keeping the knowledge

  • A lab handbook that each batch improves
  • A project library: every project leaves its code, wiring, photos, a short video and lessons learned
  • Ready-made lesson plans and prompt cards seniors can teach from
  • Train-the-mentor sessions each semester, run by us until the committee can run them

Recognition

  • Skill levels: Maker → Builder → Field engineer → Mentor; Mentor level needs a set number of sessions taught
  • Certificates and letters of recommendation from the college and AgriSense
  • Mentoring counted towards co-curricular credit, RAWE and ELP
  • A mentor-of-the-year award at demo day
  • Alumni and student founders return as guest mentors and offer internships

The lab

Spaces and equipment

Build bench

  • 6 workstations for teams of two
  • Microcontroller kits (ESP32 / ESP8266), breadboards, cables
  • Soldering stations, multimeters, bench power supplies, a basic oscilloscope
  • Student laptops (own) with free tools; lab seats for an AI assistant

Sensor and radio library

  • Temperature-humidity, soil moisture, leaf wetness, light, rain, water level and flow, pH/EC, soil NPK
  • Relays, solenoid valves, small pumps
  • WiFi, LoRa, 4G/GSM and Bluetooth modules; a solar power kit

Living lab on the college farm

  • A weather and temperature-humidity station
  • A soil moisture network in one crop block
  • A drip block with a smart irrigation controller
  • A pump with flow monitoring and remote control
  • A storage-room monitor (onion or grain); polyhouse or nursery corner if available

Product studio

  • 3D printer for enclosures and brackets
  • Sample circuit boards, enclosures and our products for teardown
  • A small test jig and flashing station, to learn batch production
  • A display wall of student products and pilot results

What we aim for

In the first two years

Year one

  • Every third-year student completes the AGR 322 workshop
  • 30–40 first- and second-year students active in the club
  • 5 student devices running on the college farm

Year two

  • 10 or more farmer pilots with student-built devices
  • A hackathon with teams from other colleges
  • At least 2 student teams applying to an incubator
