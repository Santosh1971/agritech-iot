Why

Why temperature and humidity matter on a farm

Air temperature and relative humidity (RH) are the two cheapest numbers to measure, and they drive many of the decisions a farmer makes. Crops, pests, fungi and animals all respond to them. A small sensor that records them every minute, day and night, turns “I think it was humid last week” into evidence you can act on.

AreaWhat the readings tell youWhat the farmer does
Fungal diseaseMost leaf diseases need long spells of high humidity. Late blight of potato and tomato spreads fast in cool (about 10–25 °C), very humid (RH above ~90 %) weather lasting many hours. Downy mildew (grapes, onion, cucurbits) and rice blast also follow humid nights.Spray a protective fungicide before the risky spell, not after the damage shows. Count “humid hours” per day as a simple risk alert.
Insect pestsInsects can’t control their body temperature, so warm weather speeds up their life cycle. Adding up daily heat (degree-days) predicts when the next generation of pests such as fruit borer or pink bollworm will appear.Time scouting, pheromone traps and sprays to when pests actually emerge, which means fewer and better-timed sprays.
IrrigationHot, dry air pulls water out of leaves and soil faster (high VPD, vapour pressure deficit). On humid, cloudy days crops use much less water.Give more water in hot, dry spells and less in humid weather. Irrigate in the early morning to lose less to evaporation, and avoid wetting leaves in the evening (disease risk).
SprayingAbove ~30–35 °C with low humidity, spray droplets evaporate before reaching the leaf and drift away. Very still, humid evenings also cause problems.Spray in cool, moderately humid hours (early morning or late evening) to save chemical and money and protect neighbours.
Polyhouse & nurseryProtected crops are sensitive to VPD: too humid gives disease and damping-off of seedlings; too dry makes plants close their pores and stop growing.Open vents or shade nets, or run foggers or fans, based on the readings instead of guesswork.
StorageOnions keep best at about 65–70 % RH; above that they sprout and rot. Potatoes in cold storage need high humidity (~90 %). Damp grain grows moulds (including aflatoxin) and weevils.Ventilate or dry the store, and take out stock at risk before it spoils.
Livestock & poultryHeat plus humidity (the temperature–humidity index, THI) stresses animals: dairy cows give less milk, and broilers above ~32 °C can die in heat waves.Start fans, foggers and extra water early, and shift feeding to cooler hours.
Flowering & extremesVery high temperatures at flowering reduce pollen and grain or fruit set (rice, tomato, pulses). Frost damages crops in hill areas.Irrigate to cool the crop, cover it, or use frost protection, all triggered by an alert.

Thresholds differ by crop, variety and region. Treat the numbers above as typical starting points and check them with your agronomy faculty or KVK for your crop.

Why

Why control a pump remotely, and why check the flow

Remote on/off

  • Farm power often comes only in fixed slots, often at night. Farmers walk to the field in the dark to start the motor: snakes, rain, lost sleep.
  • Starting and stopping from a phone saves those trips and lets you water on a schedule.

Flow feedback: “is water really coming?”

  • A relay turning ON does not prove water is flowing. The well may be dry, the pipe may have an air lock, or a phase may have failed.
  • A flow sensor confirms it. If there is no flow within a few seconds, switch off to protect the motor from dry running.
  • It also counts litres, so you know how much water each field got, which is the basis of drip and fertigation schedules.

Project A

TH monitor: temperature & humidity

ESP8266 (Wemos D1 mini) · TH monitor unit

A ready-built unit with a DHT22 sensor (temperature and humidity) and a DS3231 real-time clock, so every reading carries the correct time. You take this unit home after the workshop.

What you’ll build

  1. See data: one line every 10 s, e.g. {"ts":"…10:30:00","t":28.4,"h":72.1}
  2. Field app (SoftAP): join TH-xxxx, password 12345678, and see big live numbers
  3. History: the device saves every reading, even through power cuts; the app downloads it and draws a chart
  4. Internet app (MQTT): live values from anywhere, plus “online/offline” status
  5. Your rule: a disease-risk or spray-window alert for your crop

Inside the unit (for the curious)

  • DHT22 data → D7
  • DS3231 clock SDA → D3, SCL → D4
  • Blue LED → D2
  • Battery with a charger/boost chip; USB for programming

Try: breathe gently on the sensor and watch the humidity jump within a few readings.

Project B

Remote pump with flow feedback

ESP32 or ESP8266 · relay · bulb · YF-S201

A relay switches a mains bulb, which stands in for the pump motor. A hall-effect flow sensor (YF-S201) gives the feedback: blow into it and its little turbine spins as if water were flowing. The device counts the pulses and shows L/min and litres. (Air is not water, so the litres aren’t accurate, but the logic is exactly the same.)

What you’ll build

  1. See data: blow into the sensor and watch the pulses, L/min and litres
  2. Switch it: type ON / OFF and the bulb follows
  3. Proof of flow: after ON, if there’s no flow within 10 s, show “NO FLOW, pump stopped” (dry-run protection). Blow to keep it running.
  4. Field app (SoftAP): a big ON/OFF button, flow, litres and status
  5. History: every run is saved: start, stop, litres, and why it stopped
  6. Internet app (MQTT): switch it and watch the flow from anywhere

Connections

  • ESP32 DevKit: relay IN → GPIO 26 · flow signal → GPIO 27 (same as Labs 2A/2B)
  • ESP8266 D1 mini: relay IN → D1 · flow signal → D5
  • Flow sensor: red → 5 V, black → GND, yellow (signal) → through a 10 kΩ / 20 kΩ divider to the pin (the sensor gives 5 V, the pin takes 3.3 V)
  • About 450 pulses = 1 litre (YF-S201, approximate)

Mains safety: the bulb side (230 V) is pre-wired by the instructor inside a closed box. Students only touch the USB and low-voltage side. Never open the box while it is plugged in.

Records

History stored inside the device

Like our FlowGuard (FG1) controller, the device keeps its own records in memory that survives power cuts. The phone doesn’t have to be connected all the time: it collects what happened whenever it comes back in range or back online.

What is stored

  • TH monitor: time, temperature and humidity, one row per minute (about 27 days fit before the oldest are dropped)
  • Pump: one row per run: start time, stop time, litres, and how it ended (you, a timer, or no-flow protection)

How the app gets it

  • In the field: join the device WiFi; the app asks for “everything since my last sync” and draws a chart
  • Over the internet: the app sends a “get history” command and the device replies with the records
  • Download as a CSV file to open in Excel or Google Sheets

Why it matters: mobile networks drop in the field. With history on the device, no reading or pump run is lost. It is also your evidence: how many hours the pump ran, how many litres each field got, and how many humid nights preceded a disease outbreak.

Say it

Prompt cards

Copy a card into Claude, or say it in your own words, in any language. Kannada, Hindi or English all work. Add details about your crop; the more specific you are, the better the result.

A1 · See data
My TH monitor (ESP8266 Wemos D1 mini, DHT22 on D7, DS3231 clock on D3/D4) is connected by USB. Program it to print temperature, humidity and time every 10 seconds, and show me the readings.
A2 · Field app
Now make it work in the field without internet: the device should create its own WiFi, and I want a simple phone screen with big temperature and humidity numbers and the time of the reading.
A3 · History
Save every reading inside the device so nothing is lost in a power cut. In the app, add a chart of the last 24 hours and a button to download all readings as a CSV file.
A4 · Internet app
I want to see the readings from home over mobile data. Make the device send them to a free MQTT broker, and give me an app that shows live values and whether the device is online.
A5 · My crop rule
I grow tomato. Add a “late blight risk” card to the app: count the hours today with humidity above 90 % and temperature between 10 and 25 °C, and turn the card red with a message in Kannada when it passes 6 hours.
B1 · See flow
I have an ESP32 with a relay on GPIO 26 and a YF-S201 flow sensor on GPIO 27. Program it to show pulses, litres per minute and total litres every second, and let me switch the relay by typing ON or OFF.
B2 · Dry-run protection
After I switch the pump ON, if there is no flow within 10 seconds, switch it OFF and show “NO FLOW – pump stopped”. Save every pump run (start, stop, litres, reason) inside the device.
B3 · Pump app
Make a phone app for the pump, first on the device’s own WiFi and then over the internet (MQTT): a big ON/OFF button, live flow and litres, a status line, and the list of past runs.
