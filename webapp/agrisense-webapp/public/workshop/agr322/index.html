AGR 322 · Contemporary Agriculture · IoT workshop

From studying IoT to building it

You have studied wireless sensors, monitoring systems, IoT devices, plant health, soil moisture, tractors and drones, and seen a commercial system at Fyllo. On Thursday you build small versions yourself: program real farm devices, see live data, and make your own phone app. No electronics or programming background needed; you tell an AI assistant in plain English what you want, and use your agriculture knowledge to decide what the device should do.

Thursday 1 October

How the day runs

10:15–10:40

Why business? Why entrepreneurship?

8 of you rated starting your own business 5 out of 5, so we start there. What a business is: a product or a service. What it takes to run one: a real problem, a customer who pays, the product, a price, a way to sell (dealers and neighbour farmers, the people farmers trust), money, a team and after-sales service.

10:40–11:05

Your ideas: will they sell, and at what price?

We take a few of your own ideas (disease alert, storage monitor, pump protection, market price) and ask: who buys it, why, at what price, and who else could pay. Making the product is only one part of the business.

Topic 5
11:05–11:25

A product is a system: input → control → output, with feedback

Everyday examples: a fan regulator (no feedback), an iron or fridge thermostat, a tank float switch that runs the pump, a rice cooker, even your own eyes, brain and hands. Farm examples: drip on a timer vs drip on soil moisture, a fogger on humidity, a pump that checks water is really flowing. Then we map one of your ideas the same way.

Meet the brain: the ESP32

Inputs and sensors, outputs and actuators, the user interface (button, LED, phone). How you program it without writing code: ask Claude, paste, build and flash from the browser.

Hands-on 1: your first programs

Blink the LED → switch it with a button → change the blink speed with the button → temperature and humidity with the time from a real-time clock → a water flow meter (blow into it) → a pump that stops itself when no water flows. Then ask Claude for your own change and flash it.

Hands-on 2: readings on your phone over Bluetooth

The ESP32 sends time, temperature, humidity and flow to your phone, and you switch the pump from the phone. Limitation: about 10 metres, one phone at a time.

Topics 1, 3

Hands-on 3: WiFi, then the internet

First the ESP32 makes its own WiFi and your phone shows a farm dashboard with pump buttons (no internet needed, but you must be nearby). Then it joins the room’s WiFi and works through the internet (MQTT), so you can watch and switch the pump from anywhere. Limitation: the field needs WiFi or internet.

Our Wireless Pump Controller: a master reads the tank level and switches pumps on two nodes across the campus. We walk a node away to test the range. Then a real product: WaterMaster, with irrigation scheduling, its mobile app and the hardware inside. Where tractors and drones fit in the same picture.

You told us

What we learned from your answers

8 of 10 of you come from farming families, and 8 of 10 want to start your own business. Farmers lose most to pests and disease, and 7 of 10 of you said farmers spray only after they see the disease. Pumps run dry, burn out and get stolen. Farmers trust input dealers and neighbours, and believe what they see working on a real farm. Thank you for the honest answers: the day is built around them.

Outcomes

After the workshop, students can

Do

  • Program a farm IoT device and read live sensor data
  • Use the device from a phone, in the field and over the internet
  • Retrieve logged data and turn it into a report
  • Describe a farm problem clearly enough for an AI assistant to build a working app

Explain

  • How temperature and humidity drive disease, pest, irrigation, spraying and storage decisions
  • Why pump control needs flow feedback
  • When to choose WiFi, LoRa, a mobile network or Bluetooth on a farm
  • Why devices store data when the network drops

Take home

  • The programs they built, and the know-how to keep building with Claude
  • Access to the browser flasher and every workshop page, online
  • Lab sheets and prompt cards at agrisenseandcontrol.in/workshop
  • Project ideas for the practical record

We bring

College arranges

  • A room with power sockets for 6 teams, and a projector
